The graphite palette, blue accent and loop illustration are our adaptation to a research index. GitHub controls the page typography and rendering. No Apple artwork, logo or font files are embedded.

The research structure comes from the companion manuscript. Visual references do not determine scientific inclusion or classification. The homepage introduces the argument, the chapter pages follow its subsection order, and the citation index gives a compact route into all referenced works.

## Five patterns applied to the homepage

**1. Begin with a reader’s problem.** Leey21 introduces purpose before its collection and makes section identities visible with meaningful emoji. The Atlas now offers four concrete starting points: a first reading, method selection, title or key lookup, and building or evaluation. Bold phrases identify the task; the linked continuation supplies the next step.

**2. Separate the levels within an entry.** The current awesome-llm-apps README combines a centered opening, short action links, category icons, italic introductions and compact linked entries. The Atlas adopts that hierarchy: a square Awesome RWM brand, concise navigation, an italic route purpose, a bold method name, a preserved reading cue and explicit Paper, Resource and Mechanism & limits links. A resource appears only when one exists in the reviewed resource data.

**3. Let readers choose depth.** Awesome-LLM places a topic directory before expandable paper lists. The Atlas retains the full four-stage arguments, mechanism comparisons, evidence requirements and neighboring-route links inside its folds. Quick lookup is visible immediately. Dense bibliographic tables remain in the paper index and chapter guides, where their comparison columns serve a clear purpose.

**4. Offer different paths through the same subject.** Prompt Engineering Guide groups guides, techniques, applications, papers and tools. That separation is useful even when the reader already knows a method name. The Atlas’s route directory groups understanding the model, grounding it in the robot, and testing or improving it. Each group connects to the existing manuscript-aligned routes without changing their scientific placements.

**5. Make a large list easy to return to.** Awesome uses a recognizable brand, spacing, short navigation and hierarchical lists. The Atlas applies those principles with original artwork and consistent category cues. Each route links back to the directory, and the footer returns to the top. The user’s final Control diagram is visible on the homepage before the expandable explanation, whose readable text distinguishes prediction use from physical execution.

## Emoji, color and typography have distinct jobs

Category icons repeat only where they help a reader match a directory link to its destination: a compass for foundations, a puzzle for representations, a target for learning and planning, and a hand for touch. They identify the subject rather than endorse a paper.

Colored squares accompany explicit entry-kind labels: 🟦 predictive use, 🟪 representation prior, 🟨 policy context, 🟧 generative context and 🟩 benchmark. The words preserve the distinction without color. These cues are not scores or maturity levels, and do not imply automatic strict closed-loop qualification.

Bold identifies a method or a navigational question. Italics introduce the reading purpose. Ordinary text carries the substantive explanation. Flowing paper entries allow descriptions and links to wrap on narrow screens, while short horizontal separators distinguish routes.

These are adoption judgments about inspected patterns, not a single best README template. Sponsor galleries, promotional sections, animation and unsupported update promises are outside the Atlas’s research structure. GitHub controls page typography and sanitizes README HTML, so the design uses native Markdown, supported HTML, emoji and original assets instead of relying on custom inline colors.

The square Awesome RWM identity and updated robot-loop illustration are original project images. The rejected numeric statistics banner remains absent. Scientific annotations, citation placements, source mappings and the eleven substantive argument chains are preserved.
